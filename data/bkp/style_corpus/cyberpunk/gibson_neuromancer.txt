The sky above the port was the color of television, tuned to a dead channel. Not that Case had ever bothered to look up at it. The matrix had its own glow, like the light from a dead sun, and looking at the real sky only reminded him of what he'd lost.

He'd spent most of his life in the sprawl, the vast chrome and neon landscape that was the combined metropolitan area of eastern seaboard. The tech was everything here – chrome dermatrodes synced to customized cyberdecks, black market neural enhancers that could fry your synapses if the calibration was off by a micron.

The Turing Registry had his profile, his DNA spread across their secure servers like digital confetti. They knew every piece of hardware he'd ever jacked into, every neural interface he'd fried in pursuit of that perfect hack. The street finds its own uses for things, that's what they said, but lately the street had been giving him nothing but static.
